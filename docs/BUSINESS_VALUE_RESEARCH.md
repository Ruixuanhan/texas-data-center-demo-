# Project Radar — Market Context and Business Value Research

## Why the timing matters

ERCOT’s April 2026 preliminary long-term forecast projected approximately **367,790 MW** of demand in the ERCOT region by 2032, versus ERCOT’s then all-time peak demand of 85,508 MW. ERCOT explicitly described the forecast as a preliminary planning input rather than a prediction of what will be built, a distinction that highlights the need to separate **announced capacity** from **credible, evidence-backed projects**.[1]

ERCOT’s April 2026 large-load update reported approximately **410 GW** of large loads seeking interconnection, of which roughly **87%** were data centers. The same update reported 2,008 active generation-interconnection requests totaling 453,562 MW as of February 28, 2026, including 60,715 MW of gas capacity.[2] ERCOT’s June 2026 batch-process explainer updated the large-load figure to more than 438,000 MW, with nearly 90% of requests from data centers.[3]

> The business problem is therefore not merely finding projects. It is determining **which prospective loads and generation assets are real, what grid stage they are in, what evidence supports the claim, and how their status changes before a buyer’s capital, siting, procurement, or transmission decision becomes irreversible.**

## Regulatory catalyst

ERCOT replaced the prior serial study paradigm for large loads because each new request could invalidate earlier work and trigger restudies, delaying completion timelines by years. The Batch Zero framework studies eligible large loads together and can classify them as base load, studied load, or excluded load. ERCOT’s June 2026 material also describes two pathways relevant to Project Radar’s priority lens: Withdrawal-Limited Private Use Networks for users bringing on-site generation, and Provisional Controllable Load Resources for users willing to be curtailed during local stress.[3]

This creates a time-sensitive intelligence need: developers and capital providers need to know which projects are likely to gain capacity, need a grid-plus-generation solution, face a future-batch delay, or have failed to cross evidence thresholds such as site control and financial security.

## Target buyers and commercial use cases

The resulting product is not just a map—it is an **evidence-backed project-origination workflow** that solves specific commercial problems for three buyer personas:

### 1. Energy developers and EPCs (Origination and siting)
Developers need to know where large loads are actually locating so they can site generation or storage nearby, avoiding transmission-constrained zones.
* **The workflow:** Filter the map for early-stage (FEL-1 / Concept) data centers. Use the evidence timeline to verify site control. Originate a co-location or power purchase agreement (PPA) conversation before the load commits to a less reliable grid connection.

### 2. Infrastructure capital and private equity (Due diligence)
Capital providers face a landscape where 438 GW of interconnection requests represent a fraction of that in actual deployed capital. They need to separate speculative queue entries from mature, de-risked projects.
* **The workflow:** Review the entity-resolution queue to ensure a target developer’s claimed portfolio isn’t double-counting the same project under different LLCs. Use the confidence-bounded stage assessment to verify that a project claiming "shovel ready" status actually possesses a signed interconnection agreement (IA) and relevant air permits.

### 3. Utilities and transmission planners (Load forecasting)
Grid operators and utilities must plan transmission upgrades years in advance, but the new batch process means they cannot rely solely on raw queue volume.
* **The workflow:** Monitor the live intelligence feed for projects shifting from Concept to FEL-2 / Pre-FEED. Use the dual-lens view to identify regions where concentrated large-load demand lacks corresponding behind-the-meter generation, signaling a high-probability transmission constraint.
